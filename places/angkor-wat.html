Angkor Wat

Siem Reap, Cambodia

The largest religious monument in the world, a masterpiece of Khmer architecture

CATEGORY Historical
BUILT 12th Century
SIZE 162.6 Hectares
UNESCO STATUS World Heritage Site

Overview

Angkor Wat is a temple complex in Cambodia and the largest religious monument in the world. Originally constructed as a Hindu temple dedicated to the god Vishnu for the Khmer Empire, it was gradually transformed into a Buddhist temple toward the end of the 12th century.

The temple is at the top of the high classical style of Khmer architecture. It has become a symbol of Cambodia, appearing on its national flag, and it is the country's prime attraction for visitors.

Angkor Wat combines two basic plans of Khmer temple architecture: the temple-mountain and the later galleried temple. It is designed to represent Mount Meru, home of the devas in Hindu mythology: within a moat more than 5 kilometers (3 mi) long and an outer wall 3.6 kilometers (2.2 mi) long are three rectangular galleries, each raised above the next. At the center of the temple stands a quincunx of towers.

History

Angkor Wat lies 5.5 kilometres (3.4 mi) north of the modern town of Siem Reap, and a short distance south and slightly east of the previous capital, which was centered at Baphuon. It is in an area of Cambodia where there is an important group of ancient structures.

802 CE

Jayavarman II declares independence from Java and establishes the Khmer Empire.

Early 12th Century

Construction of Angkor Wat begins under King Suryavarman II, who reigned from 1113 to 1150.

1177 CE

Angkor is sacked by the Chams, enemies of the Khmer.

Late 12th Century

King Jayavarman VII establishes a new capital and state temple nearby (Angkor Thom and the Bayon respectively), but Angkor Wat remains an important monument.

14th-15th Century

Angkor Wat gradually transforms from Hindu to Buddhist use as Theravada Buddhism becomes the dominant religion in Cambodia.

Mid-15th Century

The Khmer Empire declines and the capital moves to Phnom Penh, but Angkor Wat remains in use as a Buddhist temple.

1863-1953

During French colonial rule, significant restoration work is undertaken at Angkor Wat.

1992

Angkor Wat is listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Architecture

Angkor Wat is an example of Khmer architecture, the Khmer Empire's national style. The original name of the temple was Vrah Viṣṇuloka or Parama Viṣṇuloka meaning "the sacred dwelling of Vishnu" (the temple was dedicated to the Hindu god Vishnu).

The temple is a representation of Mount Meru, the home of the gods according to Hindu mythology: the central quincunx of towers symbolizes the five peaks of the mountain, and the walls and moat represent the surrounding mountain ranges and ocean.

Key architectural features include:

  • Outer Wall and Moat: The temple is surrounded by a 190-meter wide moat and a perimeter wall measuring 1,300 by 1,500 meters.
  • Central Structure: The temple stands on a raised terrace and consists of three rectangular galleries rising to a central tower.
  • Bas-Reliefs: The outer gallery contains bas-reliefs extending for nearly 600 meters, including the famous depiction of the Churning of the Ocean of Milk.
  • Central Towers: The five central towers are arranged in a quincunx, symbolizing the five peaks of Mount Meru.
  • Orientation: Unlike most Khmer temples, Angkor Wat is oriented to the west, traditionally associated with death in Hindu culture.

Travel Tips

  • Arrive early (before sunrise) to witness the spectacular view of the sun rising behind Angkor Wat.
  • Hire a licensed guide for better understanding of the temple's history and symbolism.
  • Bring plenty of water and protect yourself from the sun - the complex offers little shade.
  • Wear comfortable shoes suitable for walking on uneven surfaces and climbing steep stairs.
  • Consider getting a multi-day pass if you plan to explore other temples in the Angkor Archaeological Park.
