  • Turn right when you reach Pontocho Alley
  • Kichi Kichi is located on the east side of Pontocho

Internet Fame and Culinary Artistry

Kichi Kichi has become an international sensation thanks to viral videos of chef-owner Motokichi Yukimura's theatrical omurice preparation. What was once a small neighborhood restaurant hidden in Kyoto's atmospheric Pontocho district is now a must-visit destination for food enthusiasts from around the world.

The restaurant specializes in omurice—a beloved yoshoku (Western-influenced Japanese) dish consisting of seasoned fried rice topped with a delicate omelette. While omurice can be found throughout Japan, Chef Yukimura's version stands apart for both its exquisite technique and showmanship.

The Art of Performance Cooking

What makes dining at Kichi Kichi a unique experience is Chef Yukimura's performative approach to cooking. Dressed in his signature bow tie and suspenders, he prepares each omurice with flair and precision at the open counter while engaging with guests.

The highlight of the performance is the creation of the omelette itself. Using a special technique that requires years of practice, Chef Yukimura cooks the eggs to a precise consistency—firm enough to hold their shape but still remarkably soft and creamy inside. With dramatic flourish, he places the golden dome of the omelette atop the rice and makes a precise incision, causing it to unfold and blanket the rice in a cascade of silky egg.

The final touch is a generous pour of rich demi-glace sauce, which complements the mild flavor of the eggs and the savory-sweet notes of the rice.

"I don't just serve omurice—I serve memories. When people see the omelette unfold, their faces light up with childlike joy. That moment of pure happiness is why I continue to perfect my craft after all these years." - Chef Motokichi Yukimura

Eastworld's Review

Food Quality: ★★★★★

The legendary omurice deserves its reputation—the textural contrast between the silky, almost custard-like eggs and the perfectly cooked rice creates a harmonious dish that elevates comfort food to an art form. The demi-glace sauce adds depth without overwhelming the delicate flavor of the eggs.

Performance Value: ★★★★★

Chef Yukimura's theatrical preparation adds immeasurable value to the dining experience. His genuine passion for his craft and interaction with guests creates moments of joy that extend far beyond the food itself.

Atmosphere: ★★★★

The tiny restaurant has a charming, intimate atmosphere. With only 10 seats around the counter, every guest has a front-row view of the action. One star deducted only for the cramped quarters, which can feel a bit tight for larger diners.

Service: ★★★★★

Despite the language barrier, Chef Yukimura and his staff provide warm, attentive service. They readily accommodate dietary restrictions when notified in advance and ensure every guest feels welcome.

Value: ★★★★

At ¥2,700 for the signature omurice, Kichi Kichi's prices are high compared to standard omurice elsewhere. However, considering the quality, craftsmanship, and entertainment value, most guests feel they received a fair value for their investment.

Overall: ★★★★★

Kichi Kichi offers a quintessential example of Japanese attention to detail and hospitality. For travelers to Kyoto seeking not just a meal but a memorable culinary experience, this tiny restaurant delivers outsized joy and satisfaction.

Tips for Visitors

  • Make reservations 1-2 months in advance; the restaurant only seats 10 people at a time
  • Request counter seating specifically for the best view of Chef Yukimura's performance
  • The restaurant is small and can be difficult to find; allow extra time to locate it
  • Consider dining early in your Kyoto trip in case you want to return for a second visit
  • While the chef speaks limited English, he communicates effectively through gestures and simple phrases
  • Photography is welcome, but be respectful of other diners' experience
  • Come with an appetite—portions are generous
  • International credit cards are accepted
