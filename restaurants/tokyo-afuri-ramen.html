The Bright, Citrusy Ramen Revolution

In a city with thousands of ramen shops, Afuri has distinguished itself with a revolutionary approach to Japan's beloved noodle soup. Founded in 2003 and named after Mount Afuri in Kanagawa Prefecture—known for its pristine water that flows from the mountains—this ramen chain has perfected a lighter, brighter style of ramen that stands in contrast to the rich, heavy tonkotsu broths that dominated the scene.

Afuri's signature innovation is the addition of yuzu citrus to their chicken-based broth, creating a refreshing, aromatic experience that has garnered them a dedicated following among both locals and tourists. Their clean, minimalist spaces with counter seating reflect the philosophy behind their ramen: simple, refined, and high-quality.

Signature Ramen & Culinary Approach

Afuri's approach to ramen starts with a chintan (clear) broth made from chicken and vegetables, meticulously strained for a clean, flavorful base. This lighter broth allows the citrus notes of yuzu to shine through, creating a refreshing profile that's especially appealing during Tokyo's hot, humid summers, though delicious year-round.

The noodles at Afuri are medium-thick and straight, with a perfect al dente texture that holds up well in their broth. Toppings are minimalist but thoughtful—typically a slice of chashu pork, half an ajitama (seasoned soft-boiled egg), nori, and bamboo shoots. The presentation is artful, with a slice of yuzu peel perched atop the bowl, releasing its aromatic oils as you eat.

Beyond their signature yuzu shio (salt) and yuzu shoyu (soy sauce) ramen, Afuri offers seasonal variations and vegan options that maintain their commitment to bright, clean flavors. Their gyoza dumplings and crispy karaage (fried chicken) make excellent side dishes to round out a meal.

"We wanted to create ramen that even people who don't typically enjoy heavy ramen could appreciate—something refreshing and clean that highlights, rather than masks, the quality of our ingredients. The addition of yuzu was revolutionary when we started, but it's become our signature that people travel across Tokyo to experience." - Afuri Representative

Menu Highlights

Afuri's menu is streamlined but offers enough variety to appeal to different tastes:

  • Yuzu Shio Ramen (¥1,200) - Their signature dish featuring a clear chicken broth with salt seasoning and yuzu citrus
  • Yuzu Shoyu Ramen (¥1,200) - A soy sauce variation of their signature ramen with a slightly deeper flavor profile
  • Yuzu Ratanmen (¥1,300) - A spicy variation with chili oil for those who enjoy some heat
  • Yuzu Tsukemen (¥1,300) - Dipping noodles with a concentrated yuzu-infused broth
  • Vegan Ramen (¥1,300) - Plant-based ramen that doesn't compromise on flavor, featuring seasonal vegetables
  • Gyoza (¥550 for 5 pieces) - Pan-fried dumplings with a crisp bottom and juicy filling
  • Karaage (¥550) - Japanese-style fried chicken with a light, crispy coating
  • Seasonal Specials - Limited-time offerings that showcase seasonal ingredients

Eastworld's Review

Broth: ★★★★★

Afuri's clear, yuzu-infused broth offers a refreshing alternative to heavier ramen styles. The balance between chicken, salt/soy, and citrus notes is masterfully executed, creating a unique flavor profile that's both complex and refreshing.

Noodles: ★★★★

The medium-thick, straight noodles have excellent texture and are served perfectly al dente. They pair well with the lighter broth, though some purists might prefer a more artisanal noodle with greater variation in texture.

Toppings: ★★★★

While minimalist compared to some ramen shops, each topping is prepared with care. The chashu is lean but tender, the eggs consistently have jammy yolks, and the bamboo shoots add pleasant texture. The yuzu peel garnish is both functional and beautiful.

Atmosphere: ★★★★

Clean, modern interiors with efficient counter service create a pleasant dining environment. The open kitchen adds to the experience, though the spaces can feel a bit clinical compared to more traditional ramen shops.

Value: ★★★★

At ¥1,200-1,500 for a bowl of ramen, Afuri sits slightly above average in pricing. However, the quality of ingredients, unique flavor profile, and overall experience justify the premium. Add-ons and side dishes are reasonably priced.

Overall: ★★★★½

Afuri has successfully carved out a unique niche in Tokyo's competitive ramen landscape. Their yuzu-infused broths offer a refreshing alternative to traditional styles, and their consistent quality across multiple locations makes them a reliable choice for both first-time visitors and ramen enthusiasts looking for something different.

Tips for Visitors

  • Visit during off-peak hours (before noon or after 2 PM) to minimize waiting time
  • Familiarize yourself with the ticket vending machine system before you go—you purchase your meal ticket first, then hand it to the staff when seated
  • Most locations have English buttons on their machines, but don't hesitate to ask staff for help if needed
  • Counter seating is the norm, making this ideal for solo diners or pairs
  • Eat your ramen promptly when served—the balance of flavors is best when hot, and the noodles can become soft if left too long
  • Consider trying both the shio (salt) and shoyu (soy sauce) variations to appreciate the subtle differences
  • If you're a spice lover, the yuzu ratanmen offers a pleasant heat that doesn't overwhelm the citrus notes
  • Vegetarians should seek out their excellent vegan ramen option, which isn't available at all ramen shops

  • Nakameguro - 1-23-1 Kamimeguro, Meguro-ku
  • Shinjuku - NEWoMan Shinjuku 2F, 4-1-6 Shinjuku

Ordering System:

Afuri uses ticket vending machines for ordering:

  1. Purchase your ticket from the vending machine near the entrance
  2. Select your preferred ramen type and any side dishes
  3. Pay using cash (machines do not accept all foreign credit cards)
  4. Take your ticket and wait to be seated if the restaurant is full
  5. Hand your ticket to the staff once seated

English Support:

  • Most Afuri locations have English options on their ticket machines
  • Staff typically have basic English abilities to assist with ordering
  • Picture menus are available at most locations

As is customary in ramen shops throughout Japan, turnover is relatively quick, and lingering after finishing your meal is not expected, especially during busy periods when others are waiting.
