  • Maisen Shinjuku Takashimaya
  • Plus additional locations in department stores and shopping centers throughout Tokyo

A Tokyo Institution in a Historic Setting

Since opening its doors in 1965, Maisen has established itself as Tokyo's definitive tonkatsu (breaded and deep-fried pork cutlet) restaurant. Located in a converted public bathhouse (sento) from the 1930s, the flagship Aoyama location combines architectural heritage with culinary excellence to create a dining experience that has attracted everyone from local salarymen to international celebrities and Japanese dignitaries.

The restaurant's high-ceilinged main dining room—with its Art Deco influences and hints of its bathing past—provides a unique atmosphere that feels simultaneously elegant and unpretentious. Beyond the distinctive setting, it's the meticulous attention to the craft of tonkatsu that has cemented Maisen's legendary status in a city where culinary perfection is the standard expectation.

The Art of Perfect Tonkatsu

What distinguishes Maisen from countless other tonkatsu establishments is its fanatical dedication to every element of the dish. The journey to the perfect cutlet begins with carefully selected pork, with options ranging from standard high-quality cuts to premium Kurobuta (Japanese black pork) from Kagoshima Prefecture. The restaurant is particularly renowned for its exclusive "Okinawa-bred Agu" and "Hirata Farm" pork varieties, which offer exceptional marbling and flavor.

The breading process involves a proprietary blend of fresh breadcrumbs that create the distinctive airy yet crisp coating that defines Maisen's cutlets. Unlike mass-produced versions, Maisen's breadcrumbs are made in-house daily, resulting in a lighter texture that doesn't absorb excess oil. The frying temperature and timing are precisely controlled to ensure each cutlet achieves the perfect balance: crispy golden exterior giving way to juicy, tender meat within.

No tonkatsu is complete without its accompanying sauce, and Maisen's signature sauce—a proprietary blend that balances sweet, tangy, and umami notes—has been perfected over decades. Each table is equipped with both sauce and Japanese mustard, allowing diners to customize to their preference.

A traditional tonkatsu set at Maisen includes the cutlet, shredded cabbage (which serves as a refreshing counterpoint to the rich pork), rice, miso soup, and Japanese pickles. The cabbage is unlimited and served with a sesame dressing that's become nearly as famous as the tonkatsu itself.

"Maisen represents the pinnacle of what can be achieved with a seemingly simple dish. Every aspect—from selecting the finest pork to controlling the temperature of the oil to the millisecond—contributes to an experience that transcends the sum of its parts. This is Japanese cooking philosophy distilled: simplicity executed with uncompromising dedication to craft." - Gourmet Tokyo Magazine

This is specialist food at its finest—a restaurant that has spent decades perfecting a single dish."

Menu Highlights

While Maisen offers a variety of pork cuts and preparations, these are the standout items that have earned the restaurant its legendary status:

  • Kurobuta Rosu Katsu Set (¥3,500) - The flagship offering featuring premium Japanese black pork loin with the ideal balance of lean meat and fat
  • Kurobuta Hire Katsu Set (¥3,200) - Tenderloin cutlet that showcases the purity of the meat with minimal fat
  • Special Agu Pork Katsu Set (¥5,000) - Limited availability; features the prized Okinawan native breed known for exceptional flavor
  • Menchi Katsu (¥2,500) - Minced meat cutlet with a uniquely soft interior texture
  • Katsu Sando (¥1,300-1,800) - Available from the takeout counter; features tonkatsu between slices of soft white bread with their signature sauce
  • Special Rosu-Hire Moriawase (¥4,200) - Combination plate featuring both loin and tenderloin cutlets for those who can't decide
  • Seasonal Special Sets - Rotating options that might include premium regional pork varieties or limited-time preparations
  • Katsu Curry (¥2,600) - Tonkatsu served with Japanese curry rice

All set meals include rice, miso soup, unlimited shredded cabbage with sesame dressing, and Japanese pickles.

Eastworld's Review

Food Quality: ★★★★★

Maisen represents the pinnacle of tonkatsu craftsmanship. Their pork selection is impeccable, the breading achieves the perfect balance of lightness and crispness, and the cutlets are consistently cooked to juicy perfection. Supporting elements like the signature sauce, cabbage with sesame dressing, and pickles are executed with equal care.

Value: ★★★★

While definitely pricier than your average tonkatsu restaurant, Maisen's quality justifies the premium. The kurobuta sets in particular deliver exceptional value given the quality of the ingredients and the skill involved. The lunch sets offer better value than dinner, with similar quality at a lower price point.

Atmosphere: ★★★★½

The converted bathhouse setting of the flagship location provides a unique backdrop that enhances the dining experience. The high ceilings and historic details create an ambiance that feels special without being stuffy. The only drawback is that the popularity means it can sometimes feel rushed, particularly during peak times.

Service: ★★★★

Service is efficient and professional, if not overly warm. Staff are accustomed to international visitors and can generally navigate language barriers effectively. During peak hours, service can feel somewhat hurried as they try to accommodate waiting diners.

Authenticity: ★★★★★

This is the definitive version of a Japanese staple prepared according to traditional methods but elevated to an art form. Maisen has been setting the standard for tonkatsu for decades and represents authentic Japanese culinary values: simplicity, quality of ingredients, and masterful technique.

Overall: ★★★★½

Maisen delivers a quintessential Tokyo dining experience by taking a humble dish and elevating it through obsessive attention to detail and commitment to quality. For anyone seeking to understand Japanese cuisine's dedication to perfecting seemingly simple foods, Maisen offers a delicious education. Even with the premium pricing and potential wait times, it remains a must-visit culinary destination in Tokyo.

Tips for Visitors

  • Timing: Arrive right when they open (11:00 AM) or during off-peak hours (2:30-5:00 PM) to minimize wait times
  • Seating: The first floor is more vibrant but busier; request second-floor seating for a slightly more relaxed experience
  • Menu Navigation: For first-timers, the kurobuta rosu (loin) set offers the definitive Maisen experience
  • Food Customization: Feel free to request more cabbage; it's unlimited and complements the rich cutlet perfectly
  • Sauce Technique: Try your first few bites without sauce to appreciate the meat quality, then experiment with different sauce amounts
  • Language: An English menu is available; staff can generally handle basic English requests
  • Takeout Option: If lines are too long, consider trying their excellent katsu sando (cutlet sandwich) from the takeout counter
  • Alternative Locations: If the flagship location is too crowded, consider visiting one of the department store branches, which maintain similar quality standards

Visitor Information

Here's what you need to know when planning your visit to Maisen:

Getting There:

  • From Omotesando Station (Tokyo Metro Ginza, Hanzomon, and Chiyoda Lines): Exit A4, walk approximately 5 minutes
  • From Gaienmae Station (Tokyo Metro Ginza Line): Exit 3, walk approximately 7 minutes
  • Look for the distinctive white building with "MAISEN" signage

Best Times to Visit:

  • Weekdays are generally less crowded than weekends
  • Opening time (11:00 AM) has a line but moves quickly
  • Late lunch (2:00-4:00 PM) typically sees shorter waits
  • Expect 30-60 minute waits during peak lunch and dinner hours on weekends

Payment Information:

  • Major credit cards accepted (Visa, Mastercard, JCB, American Express)
  • Cash (Japanese yen) is always accepted
  • Some locations accept digital payment methods like Suica/Pasmo
  • No tipping necessary (as is standard in Japan)

Accessibility:

The historic main building presents some accessibility challenges:

  • Main entrance has several steps with no ramp
  • First floor dining area is accessible once inside
  • Second floor is accessed by stairs only (no elevator)
  • Department store locations typically offer better accessibility

While Maisen is popular with tourists, it remains an authentic Japanese dining establishment. Respectful behavior and patience during busy periods are appreciated, as is customary in Japan.
