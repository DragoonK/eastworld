From Roppongi Station:

  • Take the Tokyo Metro Hibiya Line or Toei Oedo Line to Roppongi Station
  • Exit at Roppongi Hills Exit
  • Walk approximately 8 minutes to Ark Hills South Tower
  • Sushi Saito is located on the first floor of the South Tower

The Pinnacle of Edomae Sushi

Sushi Saito represents the pinnacle of Tokyo's sushi scene, with three Michelin stars and a perennial spot on the World's 50 Best Restaurants list. Chef Takashi Saito has created what many sushi connoisseurs consider to be the finest sushi restaurant in the world.

Located in the upscale Ark Hills complex in Roppongi, the restaurant is surprisingly understated from the outside. The interior is equally minimal—an intimate counter with just 8 seats, allowing Chef Saito to personally serve each guest with meticulous attention to detail.

The Omakase Experience

Sushi Saito exclusively offers omakase (chef's selection) courses. The meal progresses through approximately 20 pieces of sushi, each a masterpiece of simplicity and technique. Chef Saito's philosophy focuses on enhancing the natural flavors of the fish rather than masking them—his rice is seasoned with a precise balance of red vinegar, and fish is aged meticulously to develop optimal flavor and texture.

What sets Saito's sushi apart is his extraordinary attention to detail: the temperature of the rice, the amount of wasabi, the precise knife cut, the gentle brushing of nikiri shoyu (a specially prepared soy sauce)—all these elements are calibrated to perfection with each piece made individually moments before being served.

"The ultimate aim of sushi is to achieve a perfect balance between the fish and the rice. My goal is for customers to experience each ingredient in its ideal state—a moment of harmony between the fish, the rice, and the timing of consumption." - Chef Takashi Saito

Atmosphere & Experience

Sushi Saito's interior is strikingly minimalist—plain beige walls, simple wooden counter, no decorations or music to distract from the food. This environment focuses all attention on the interaction between the chef and the ingredients.

The experience is intimate and engaging. Chef Saito personally prepares and serves each piece directly onto your plate, carefully timing the progression to ensure optimal temperature and texture. Despite his renowned status, he maintains a warm, unpretentious demeanor, often sharing insights about the day's fish selection or traditional preparation techniques.

Eastworld's Review

Food Quality: ★★★★★

Perfection in simplicity. Chef Saito's sushi represents the absolute pinnacle of Edomae-style sushi, with impeccable rice, fish selection, and technique. The progression of flavors throughout the meal is masterfully orchestrated.

Value: ★★★★

While certainly expensive (approximately ¥40,000-50,000 per person), the extraordinary quality, renowned status, and intimate experience make it justified for what is widely considered one of the finest sushi experiences in the world.

Atmosphere: ★★★★★

The minimalist space creates the perfect environment to focus entirely on the food. The counter seating arrangement fosters an intimate connection with both the chef and fellow diners.

Service: ★★★★★

Chef Saito himself serves each piece with precise timing and thoughtful explanation. Staff are attentive but unobtrusive, maintaining a peaceful atmosphere.

Overall: ★★★★★

Sushi Saito represents the pinnacle of Tokyo's sushi scene and one of the world's most extraordinary dining experiences. For serious food enthusiasts, it's a pilgrimage worth making—if you can secure a reservation.

Tips for Visitors

  • Reservations are notoriously difficult to secure and typically require booking through a high-end hotel concierge or established regular
  • Japanese language ability is helpful but not essential—Chef Saito can communicate adequately in basic English
  • The entire meal takes approximately 1.5-2 hours
  • Photography is permitted but should be discreet and respectful
  • Punctuality is essential—arriving late may result in forfeiting your reservation
  • Traditional sushi etiquette applies—eat each piece shortly after it's served, and use hands rather than chopsticks for nigiri
