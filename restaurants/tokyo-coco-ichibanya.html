Japan's Beloved Curry Chain: A National Institution

Since opening its first restaurant in 1978, CoCo Ichibanya (commonly called "CoCoIchi" by locals) has grown to become Japan's largest curry restaurant chain and a beloved national institution. What began as a small shop in Aichi Prefecture has expanded to over 1,300 locations across Japan and has even spread internationally to countries including the United States, China, Thailand, and the United Kingdom.

While Japan is renowned for its traditional cuisine, curry rice (karē raisu) holds a special place in the country's modern food culture. Introduced via British naval officers in the late 19th century, Japanese-style curry has evolved into a distinct national dish that bears little resemblance to its Indian origins. CoCo Ichibanya has been instrumental in popularizing this Japanese adaptation, serving a mild, thick curry sauce that's sweeter and less spicy than its South Asian counterpart.

The Ultimate Customizable Dining Experience

What truly sets CoCo Ichibanya apart from other curry restaurants is its unparalleled approach to customization. The chain revolutionized the fast-casual dining scene in Japan by allowing customers to personalize virtually every aspect of their meal. This "have it your way" philosophy has made CoCo Ichibanya particularly popular with both locals and tourists who appreciate the ability to tailor their curry experience to their exact preferences.

The ordering system is remarkably flexible, allowing diners to select:

  • Spice Level: From mild (level 1) to extremely spicy (level 10), with the option to go even hotter with special "danger" levels that require signing a waiver
  • Rice Portion: From 200g to 600g, with options to increase or decrease by 100g increments
  • Main Toppings: Choose from over 30 options including pork cutlet (katsu), hamburger, chicken, seafood, vegetables, cheese, and more
  • Extra Toppings: Add-ons like boiled eggs, natto (fermented soybeans), corn, spinach, or additional cheese
  • Curry Sauce Variations: Options like beef keema curry, vegetable curry, seafood curry, or "dragon" curry (extra spicy)

This customization system means there are literally thousands of possible combinations, ensuring that even regular customers can try something new with each visit. This adaptability also makes CoCo Ichibanya an excellent choice for groups with varied dietary preferences or spice tolerances.

"Our philosophy has always been that curry should be for everyone. By letting customers create their perfect dish—whether they want it mild or fiery hot, light or hearty, vegetarian or meat-focused—we've turned a simple comfort food into an experience that people can personalize and make their own." - CoCo Ichibanya representative

Menu Highlights

While the customization options are nearly endless, these are some of the most popular combinations at CoCo Ichibanya:

  • Pork Cutlet (Katsu) Curry (¥990) - The quintessential Japanese curry experience, featuring a crispy panko-breaded pork cutlet
  • Hamburger Curry (¥890) - A juicy beef patty atop curry rice, often ordered with cheese as an add-on
  • Chicken Cutlet Curry (¥990) - Similar to the pork version but with chicken breast
  • Vegetable Curry (¥790) - A medley of seasonal vegetables, popular with vegetarians (though note that the standard curry roux contains meat stock)
  • Ebi Fry Curry (¥1,090) - Featuring large breaded and fried shrimp
  • Cheese Curry (¥890) - The standard curry topped with melted cheese
  • Spinach Curry (¥890) - Curry with a generous portion of spinach mixed in, adding nutrition and a different texture
  • Half & Half (¥990+) - Can't decide? Get two different curry varieties in one plate
  • Omu-curry (¥1,090) - Curry wrapped in a fluffy omelet
  • Kids Set (¥490) - Smaller portion with mild curry, popular with children and those wanting a lighter meal

Side dishes include options like fried chicken, salads, and additional rice. Beverage options typically include soft drinks, beer, and free water.

Eastworld's Review

Food Quality: ★★★★

CoCo Ichibanya serves consistently good Japanese curry that hits all the right comfort food notes. While it won't compete with specialty curry restaurants for depth of flavor, the quality is impressively reliable across all locations. The ingredients are fresh, portions are generous, and the customization options ensure everyone finds something to enjoy.

Value: ★★★★★

Few restaurants in Japan offer this combination of portion size, quality, and affordability. Most meals cost between ¥800-1,500, with the option to adjust rice quantity to suit your appetite. The ability to create a filling, satisfying meal at this price point in expensive urban areas like Tokyo makes CoCo Ichibanya an excellent value proposition.

Customization: ★★★★★

This is where CoCo Ichibanya truly excels. The ability to customize spice levels, portion sizes, toppings, and curry types creates a personalized dining experience that few restaurant chains can match. This flexibility makes it equally suitable for spice enthusiasts, conservative eaters, and those with specific dietary preferences.

Atmosphere: ★★★

The restaurants are clean, efficient, and functional, though not particularly memorable in terms of ambiance. The setting is casual and comfortable but designed for relatively quick turnover rather than lingering meals. Some newer locations have more modern interiors, while older branches can feel a bit dated.

Accessibility: ★★★★½

With English menus, picture-based ordering systems, and ubiquitous locations near major tourist areas, CoCo Ichibanya is exceptionally accessible to international visitors. The straightforward ordering process and familiar comfort food concept make it an easy entry point to Japanese dining for travelers experiencing language barriers or food anxiety.

Overall: ★★★★

CoCo Ichibanya won't be the most memorable culinary experience of your Japan trip, but it fills an important niche in the country's dining landscape. When you need a reliable, affordable, and satisfying meal between more adventurous dining experiences, CoCo Ichibanya delivers consistently good Japanese curry with unmatched customization options. It's a genuine local favorite that offers insight into everyday Japanese dining habits.

Tips for Visitors

  • Spice Levels: Start conservative with the spice unless you're very heat-tolerant. Level 3 is medium spicy by Japanese standards, while levels 7-10 can be challenging even for spice enthusiasts
  • Ordering Process: At most locations, you order and pay at your table. Some branches use ticket vending machines at the entrance
  • Rice Portions: The standard 300g serving is quite large. Consider the 200g option if you're a light eater or want to save room for other foods
  • Vegetarian Options: While vegetable curry is available, the standard curry roux contains meat stock. Some larger locations in tourist areas offer a plant-based curry option
  • Popular Add-ons: Cheese, boiled egg, and spinach are popular and inexpensive additions that enhance the curry experience
  • Lunch Specials: Many locations offer discounted sets during lunch hours (typically until 3:00 PM)
  • Free Toppings: Fukujinzuke (pickled vegetables) and rakkyo (pickled scallions) are available at no charge - look for them at the condiment station
  • Busy Times: City center locations can get crowded during peak lunch (12:00-1:00 PM) and dinner (7:00-8:00 PM) times

Visitor Information

Here's what you need to know when planning a visit to CoCo Ichibanya:

Finding Locations:

  • Look for the distinctive yellow and brown logo featuring a stylized plate of curry
  • Most major train stations and shopping districts have at least one branch nearby
  • Google Maps reliably shows all locations with accurate operating hours
  • Most tourist apps like Japan Travel by Navitime include CoCo Ichibanya locations

Ordering Guide:

  • Most locations offer English menus—just ask the staff
  • Picture menus make selections clear even without language skills
  • Spice levels are clearly marked with numbers (1-10)
  • Customization options are typically displayed on the menu with corresponding prices
  • Some branches use vending machine ordering, while others take orders at the table

Seating & Wait Times:

  • Smaller branches may have counter-only seating
  • Larger locations offer tables and sometimes private booths
  • During peak hours, expect 5-15 minute waits at popular branches
  • Food typically arrives within 5-10 minutes after ordering
  • Solo diners are common and easily accommodated

Payment Information:

  • Cash is accepted at all locations
  • Most branches accept major credit cards (Visa, Mastercard, JCB)
  • Some locations accept electronic payments like Suica/Pasmo transit cards
  • Prices include tax
  • No tipping is necessary, as is standard in Japan

As a casual dining establishment, CoCo Ichibanya doesn't require reservations and operates on a first-come, first-served basis. The dining experience is designed to be efficient but not rushed, with most meals taking 30-45 minutes from entry to exit.
