The Hot Pot Empire's Flagship Experience

Haidilao has grown from a small restaurant in Sichuan Province to become China's most recognizable hot pot chain, with hundreds of locations across the country and internationally. Yet despite this massive expansion, the Beijing Wangfujing location maintains the exceptional standards and personalized service that made the brand famous.

More than just a meal, Haidilao offers a comprehensive dining experience that showcases the interactive, social nature of hot pot—a style of cooking where diners cook fresh ingredients in a simmering pot of flavored broth at their table. While the concept is traditional, Haidilao's approach to service and atmosphere has revolutionized China's restaurant industry.

Legendary Service Excellence

What truly sets Haidilao apart from other hot pot establishments is their unwavering commitment to customer service. The chain has become legendary for going above and beyond in ways that have transformed expectations across China's service industry:

Complimentary Manicures

While waiting for a table, guests can enjoy free manicures and shoe polishing services.

Phone Protectors

Diners receive plastic covers to protect their phones from splashes during the meal.

Family Support

Families with children receive special attention, including childcare assistance during the meal.

These thoughtful touches exemplify the company's philosophy that service, rather than just food, creates loyal customers. Staff undergo intensive training to anticipate needs—from offering hair ties to long-haired diners to providing reading glasses for those who forgot theirs. For solo diners, they even provide a large stuffed animal companion to sit across the table.

"What makes us different is our dedication to solving problems for our guests. We don't just serve food; we create memories. Everyone who walks through our doors is treated like family, and that's why people keep coming back. The hot pot itself is important, but the experience surrounding it is what truly defines Haidilao." - Zhang Yong, Founder of Haidilao

Signature Broth Selection

At the heart of the Haidilao experience is the selection of broths, which form the base for cooking all ingredients. The restaurant offers various flavors, often in a divided pot that allows diners to try multiple options simultaneously:

Sichuan Mala

The signature numbing-spicy broth features Sichuan peppercorns, dried chilies, and a proprietary blend of over 20 herbs and spices. The result is the distinctive ma-la (numbing-spicy) sensation that Sichuan cuisine is famous for.

Mushroom

A vegetarian-friendly option with a rich umami flavor derived from several varieties of dried mushrooms, including shiitake and porcini. The clear broth becomes increasingly flavorful as ingredients cook in it.

Tomato

A tangy, slightly sweet broth made with fresh tomatoes, ideal for those who prefer non-spicy options or as a palate-cleansing contrast to the spicy Sichuan broth. Popular with children and those new to hot pot.

The Sauce Bar Experience

Haidilao's extensive sauce bar is a destination in itself, offering dozens of ingredients that allow diners to create personalized dipping sauces. The typical sauce-making process involves selecting a base (such as sesame paste, soy sauce, or vinegar) and then adding various elements:

  • Aromatics: Minced garlic, green onions, cilantro
  • Spices: Chili oil, Sichuan pepper oil, white pepper
  • Flavoring Agents: Sesame oil, oyster sauce, fermented bean curd
  • Textural Elements: Crushed peanuts, fried garlic chips, preserved vegetables

Staff members are happy to recommend combinations or even prepare a sauce for you based on your preferences. This customization aspect adds another interactive dimension to the meal and allows each diner to tailor the experience to their taste.

Fresh Ingredients Selection

Haidilao's menu is extensive, featuring dozens of items that can be cooked in the hot pot. The focus is on ultra-fresh ingredients, with meat sliced paper-thin for quick cooking and vegetables selected for their ability to absorb the flavors of the broth:

  • Premium Meats (¥48-88): Australian wagyu beef, lamb shoulder, pork belly
  • Seafood (¥38-68): Live shrimp, fish fillets, scallops, crab sticks
  • Handmade Specialties (¥28-48): Shrimp paste, beef and cuttlefish balls, tofu puffs
  • Vegetables (¥18-28): Lotus root, crown daisy, Chinese cabbage, winter melon
  • Mushrooms (¥18-28): Enoki, king oyster, shiitake, wood ear
  • Noodles (¥18-28): Hand-pulled noodles, sweet potato noodles, rice noodles

One of the highlights of dining at Haidilao is their famous noodle dance, where a staff member performs an elaborate demonstration of pulling noodles by hand, stretching the dough with theatrical flair before adding the fresh noodles to your hot pot.

Eastworld's Review

Food Quality: ★★★★

Haidilao consistently offers premium ingredients that are noticeably fresher than many competitors. Their broths are complex and well-balanced, particularly the signature Sichuan mala option. While not the most innovative hot pot in Beijing, their execution of classics is nearly flawless.

Authenticity: ★★★★

The hot pot tradition is honored in its essential elements, with broths and ingredients that respect regional Chinese flavors. The theatrical service elements are modern innovations but don't detract from the authentic culinary experience.

Value: ★★★

At ¥150-250 per person, Haidilao is more expensive than many local hot pot restaurants. However, the combination of ingredient quality, portion sizes, and extraordinary service creates fair value for the price. First-time visitors will find the experience worth the premium.

Atmosphere: ★★★★

The restaurant expertly balances energetic communal dining with comfort and privacy. Tables are well-spaced, noise levels are managed despite the bustling environment, and the decor is modern but not ostentatious. The Wangfujing location features particularly spacious seating and excellent ventilation systems.

Service: ★★★★★

Service is where Haidilao truly excels beyond all expectations. The attentiveness, resourcefulness, and genuine warmth of the staff create a dining experience that's memorable for reasons beyond the food. For international visitors, their patient explanations and English-speaking staff make hot pot accessible even to novices.

Overall: ★★★★

Haidilao offers an ideal introduction to Chinese hot pot for visitors while maintaining enough quality and authenticity to satisfy locals. The combination of good food and exceptional service makes it a reliable choice for experiencing this iconic Chinese dining tradition without intimidation.

Tips for Visitors

  • Download the Haidilao app or arrive early to minimize wait times (30-60 minute waits are common during peak hours)
  • For first-timers, servers are happy to recommend cooking times for different ingredients—follow their guidance
  • The divided pot (鸳鸯锅 yuan yang guo) allows you to try multiple broths; typically half-spicy and half-mild is recommended
  • Wear casual clothes as there may be splashing despite aprons and bibs provided
  • Pace yourself when ordering—you can always add more items later
  • Visit the sauce bar early in your meal to prepare your dipping sauces
  • Don't miss the hand-pulled noodles demonstration near the end of your meal
  • Ask staff to adjust spice levels to your preference—they can customize the broth
  • Be prepared for a 2-3 hour dining experience to fully enjoy the hot pot process
