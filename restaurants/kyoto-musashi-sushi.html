How to Get There

From Shijo Station or Kawaramachi Station:

  • Walk approximately 5 minutes to Nishiki Market
  • Enter from the eastern end near Takakura-dori
  • Walk about 3/4 of the way through the market
  • Look for the restaurant on the left side with the distinctive yellow signage
  • You can often identify it by the line of people waiting outside

Nishiki Market's Beloved Sushi Institution

Located in the heart of Kyoto's famed "Kitchen of Kyoto" - Nishiki Market - Musashi Sushi has established itself as one of the city's most popular and affordable sushi destinations. This no-frills conveyor belt sushi restaurant (known as "kaiten-zushi" in Japanese) offers an authentic, budget-friendly Japanese dining experience that attracts both locals and tourists alike.

Unlike high-end sushi establishments that might intimidate first-time visitors to Japan, Musashi embraces a casual, accessible approach to sushi dining. The restaurant's modest storefront might be easy to miss amid the bustling market stalls, but the consistent line of patrons outside marks its location. Inside, the energetic atmosphere, rapid turnover of fresh plates, and the hypnotic movement of the conveyor belt create a quintessentially Japanese dining experience.

The Conveyor Belt Experience

Musashi operates on the simple concept that has made conveyor belt sushi a Japanese cultural icon. Seated at a counter surrounding a rotating conveyor belt, diners simply grab plates of sushi as they pass by. Each plate is color-coded to indicate its price, though at Musashi, most plates are uniformly priced at ¥137 (tax included) - roughly $1 per plate. This straightforward pricing makes it especially easy for visitors to keep track of their spending.

While some conveyor belt sushi chains rely heavily on automation, Musashi maintains the human touch. Behind the counter, skilled chefs continuously prepare fresh sushi, placing it directly onto the conveyor belt. If you don't see what you're craving on the belt, you can order directly from the chefs who will make it to order and hand it to you - a system that ensures freshness and customization despite the fast-paced environment.

The restaurant's relatively small size (seating around 30 people) contributes to its intimate feel and ensures rapid turnover of sushi, meaning the fish rarely sits on the belt for long. The constant movement of the plates, the calls between staff members, and the satisfaction of assembling your own unique meal create a dynamic dining experience that's as much about the atmosphere as it is about the food.

"In Kyoto's Nishiki Market, where centuries-old specialty food shops operate alongside modern eateries, Musashi Sushi represents the perfect middle ground - traditional Japanese cuisine presented in an accessible, modern format at prices that allow travelers to enjoy quality seafood without breaking their budget." - Kyoto Food Guide

Menu Highlights

While the selection rotates based on seasonal availability, some staple offerings at Musashi include:

  • Maguro (Tuna) - Available in various cuts, from lean akami to fatty otoro when in season
  • Sake (Salmon) - Fresh and often available in both regular and aburi (flame-seared) preparations
  • Hamachi (Yellowtail) - A buttery, rich option that's particularly popular
  • Tamago (Egg) - Sweet Japanese omelette, a good option for those new to sushi
  • Saba (Mackerel) - A local Kyoto specialty, lightly pickled and full of flavor
  • Ika (Squid) - Tender slices with a subtle sweetness
  • Unagi (Freshwater Eel) - Brushed with a sweet soy glaze and flame-grilled
  • Gunkan Maki - "Battleship" style sushi topped with ingredients like ikura (salmon roe) or negitoro (minced fatty tuna with green onion)
  • Miso Soup - Available to order separately to complement your sushi selection
  • Green Tea - Complimentary with your meal, as is customary in Japan

Eastworld's Review

Food Quality: ★★★★

For a budget-friendly conveyor belt sushi restaurant, Musashi delivers impressive quality. The fish is fresh, cuts are generous, and the rice is properly seasoned. While it won't compete with high-end sushi establishments, it consistently exceeds expectations for its price point.

Value: ★★★★★

This is where Musashi truly shines. At ¥137 per plate regardless of the type of sushi, it offers exceptional value in an increasingly expensive city. A filling meal of 8-10 plates will cost less than ¥1,500 ($10), making it one of Kyoto's best food bargains.

Atmosphere: ★★★½

The bustling, somewhat cramped atmosphere is part of the authentic experience, though not everyone will appreciate the close quarters and fast-paced environment. The location within Nishiki Market adds to the local feel, but expect some noise and potential waits.

Service: ★★★½

Despite language barriers, the staff is efficient and accommodating. The self-service nature of conveyor belt sushi means minimal interaction is required, but chefs are responsive when custom orders are requested.

Authenticity: ★★★★

Musashi offers an authentic Japanese kaiten-zushi experience in a market setting frequented by locals—a combination that provides cultural insight along with a satisfying meal. The restaurant hasn't been overly modified to cater to tourists, maintaining its genuine character.

Overall: ★★★★

Musashi Sushi exemplifies what budget travel in Japan can be at its best—authentic, satisfying, and accessible. It's not the pinnacle of Japanese cuisine, but it offers a genuine culinary experience that balances quality and value perfectly. For travelers exploring Nishiki Market, it's an ideal lunch spot that delivers both sustenance and cultural experience.

Tips for Visitors

  • Timing is Key: Visit during off-peak hours (before noon or between 2-5 PM) to minimize wait times
  • Plate Etiquette: Stack your finished plates at your place setting; staff will count them at the end of your meal to calculate your bill
  • Don't Be Shy: If you don't see what you want on the belt, you can order directly from the chefs
  • Water and Tea: Self-service tea powder and hot water are available at each seat
  • Wasabi Warning: Most nigiri already has wasabi applied between the fish and rice; additional wasabi is available if desired
  • Eating Technique: Traditional sushi etiquette suggests eating nigiri in one bite, fish-side down on your tongue
  • Payment: Cash is preferred, though some credit cards are accepted. Have yen ready for a smoother experience
  • Tipping: As with all establishments in Japan, no tipping is expected or necessary

Visitor Information

Here's what you need to know when planning your visit to Musashi Sushi:

Best Times to Visit:

  • Weekdays tend to be less crowded than weekends
  • Early opening (11:00 AM) is a good time to avoid lines
  • Late afternoon (2:30-5:00 PM) is typically less busy
  • The restaurant is busiest during standard lunch (12:00-1:30 PM) and dinner (6:00-8:00 PM) times

What to Expect:

  • Line formation outside the restaurant (staff may provide approximate wait times)
  • Compact seating with counter spots around the conveyor belt
  • Fast turnover - most diners finish within 30-45 minutes
  • Limited English spoken by staff, but picture menus are available
  • Self-service green tea powder and hot water dispensers at each seat

Combine With:

Musashi's location in Nishiki Market makes it ideal to combine with:

  • Exploring the 400+ year old market (allow 1-2 hours for browsing)
  • Shopping in nearby Teramachi and Shinkyogoku covered shopping arcades
  • Visiting Nishiki Tenmangu Shrine at the western end of the market
  • Walking to Gion district (15 minutes east) for afternoon tea or evening geisha spotting

As is typical in Japan, the restaurant appreciates efficient dining, especially during busy periods. Once you've finished eating, it's customary to pay and depart to make room for waiting guests.
