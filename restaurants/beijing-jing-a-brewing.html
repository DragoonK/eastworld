Beijing's Craft Beer Revolution

In a city known more for its imperial history than its brewing culture, Jing-A Brewing Co. has spearheaded nothing short of a craft beer revolution in Beijing. Founded in 2012 by expats Alex Acker and Kristian Li, what began as a home brewing experiment has grown into one of China's most influential craft breweries, known for its innovative takes on traditional styles and unique beers that incorporate Chinese ingredients and brewing traditions.

Named after the historic "Jing-A" license plate designation for Beijing vehicles, the brewery proudly celebrates its connection to the Chinese capital while pushing the boundaries of what Chinese craft beer can be. Their flagship taproom in the trendy Sanlitun area has become a gathering place for both expats and Chinese beer enthusiasts, creating a cultural bridge through shared appreciation of quality brewing.

The Brewery: Where East Meets West

Walking into Jing-A's flagship location, visitors immediately sense the brewery's unique positioning at the crossroads of Western craft brewing tradition and Chinese cultural context. The industrial-chic design features exposed brick, concrete floors, and brewing equipment visible behind glass walls, creating a contemporary atmosphere that still feels distinctly Beijing.

The space is divided into several areas, including a main bar, communal tables, a more intimate seating section, and a seasonal outdoor patio. The decor includes subtle nods to Beijing culture—vintage posters, artistic representations of hutong architecture, and creative uses of traditional Chinese design elements reimagined in a modern context.

"We didn't want to create just another generic craft brewery that could be anywhere in the world. Beijing is in our name and our DNA. We're constantly thinking about how to integrate local ingredients, flavors, and cultural references into our beers in ways that are authentic rather than gimmicky. When people visit our taproom, we want them to experience something that could only exist in China's capital." - Alex Acker, Co-founder of Jing-A Brewing Co.

Innovative Beers with Chinese Character

What truly sets Jing-A apart in the global craft beer landscape is its creative integration of Chinese ingredients and brewing traditions. The brewery regularly collaborates with local tea producers, spice merchants, and even traditional Chinese medicine practitioners to source unique ingredients that become the highlights of their signature brews.

Experimental and Seasonal Releases

Beyond their core lineup, Jing-A has gained a reputation for boundary-pushing experimental brews that often appear for limited runs. These have included:

  • Hutong Clan Imperial Stout - Aged in Chinese baijiu liquor barrels, creating a complex beer with notes of dark chocolate, licorice, and the distinctive sorghum character of baijiu
  • Qu Brew Sour Ale - Using traditional Chinese fermentation starters (qu) to create a unique mixed-fermentation sour beer
  • Jing-A x Mijiaya Historical Ale - A collaboration with archaeologists to recreate a 5,000-year-old Chinese beer recipe discovered in Shaanxi Province
  • Beijing Bikini Watermelon Wheat - A summer seasonal celebrating the Beijing tradition of men rolling up their shirts to cool off in summer heat
  • Airpocalypse Double IPA - A tongue-in-cheek beer whose price fluctuates based on Beijing's daily air quality readings

Brewery Tours

Jing-A offers guided brewery tours at their production facility in Tongzhou District every Saturday at 2 PM. The 90-minute tour includes:

  • A walkthrough of the brewing process
  • Background on Jing-A's brewing philosophy and history
  • Guided tasting of 5 different beers
  • Souvenir Jing-A glass
  • 10% discount on merchandise

Cost: ¥150 per person (approximately $22 USD)
Advance reservation required: tours@jingabrewing.com

Gastropub Fare with Global Influences

While beer is the primary focus, Jing-A also offers a comprehensive food menu that complements their brewing program. The kitchen team takes a similarly innovative approach, creating gastropub fare that blends Western comfort food with Chinese influences and ingredients.

Xinjiang Cumin Lamb Burger

A fusion favorite combining the spiced lamb of China's Xinjiang region with a Western-style burger format, topped with yogurt sauce, pickled vegetables, and served on a toasted sesame bun.
¥85 ($12)

Sichuan Spice Dry Rub Wings

¥65 ($9)

Hutong Pizzas

Thin-crust pizzas with unconventional toppings that reference Beijing cuisine, such as the "Peking Duck Pizza" with hoisin sauce, cucumber, and crispy duck.
¥95-120 ($14-18)

The menu includes vegetarian and vegan options, and the kitchen is willing to accommodate dietary restrictions when possible. Food is served until one hour before closing, with a limited late-night menu available on weekends.

Community and Events

Jing-A has built more than just a brewery—they've created a community hub that hosts regular events bringing together beer enthusiasts, expats, and locals. The brewery has become an important cultural bridge in Beijing, creating shared experiences through a mutual appreciation of craft beer.

Monthly: Every Third Thursday

8x8 Brewing Project Sessions

A collaborative event featuring eight different brewers (both professional and skilled homebrewers) presenting special small-batch creations for tasting and discussion.

Annual: May

Jing-A Beer Festival

An annual celebration bringing together craft breweries from across China and Asia for a weekend of tastings, food pairings, live music, and brewing workshops.

Weekly: Wednesdays

Beer School

Educational sessions focusing on different beer styles, brewing techniques, or ingredients, with guided tastings and Q&A with the brewing team.

The Rise of Chinese Craft Beer

Jing-A's success is emblematic of the broader craft beer movement taking root in China's major cities. In just over a decade, Beijing has gone from having virtually no craft beer scene to hosting dozens of microbreweries, with Jing-A leading the charge in terms of innovation, quality, and cultural integration.

The brewery's influence extends beyond Beijing as they've helped cultivate a nationwide network of craft brewers through collaborations, festivals, and knowledge sharing. They've also gained international recognition, with their beers appearing at festivals in the United States, Europe, and throughout Asia.

For visitors to Beijing, Jing-A offers a window into contemporary Chinese urban culture—one that respects tradition while enthusiastically embracing global influences and reimagining them in a uniquely Chinese context.

Eastworld's Review

Beer Quality: ★★★★★

Jing-A produces craft beers that stand up to international standards while establishing a uniquely Chinese identity. Their core lineup demonstrates technical precision and consistency, while their experimental offerings showcase creativity and risk-taking that pushes the envelope of what craft beer can be in China.

Food Quality: ★★★★

The gastropub menu successfully complements the beer selection with well-executed fusion dishes that incorporate Chinese flavors in approachable formats. While the focus is clearly on the brewery operation, the food program doesn't feel like an afterthought, offering thoughtful pairings and high-quality execution.

Authenticity: ★★★★★

Despite being founded by expats, Jing-A has successfully created an experience that feels genuinely rooted in Beijing. Their incorporation of Chinese ingredients, brewing traditions, and cultural references goes beyond surface-level appropriation to demonstrate real engagement with and respect for local culture.

Value: ★★★★

At ¥50-80 for a pint, prices are higher than local Chinese beers but comparable to craft beer prices in major cities worldwide. The quality justifies the cost, though budget-conscious travelers should look for their weekday happy hour offerings to maximize value.

Atmosphere: ★★★★★

The taproom strikes a perfect balance between contemporary industrial-chic brewery aesthetics and subtle Chinese design elements. The space works equally well for casual drinks, business meetings, or social gatherings, with different seating areas accommodating various needs.

Service: ★★★★

Staff are knowledgeable about the beer offerings and capable of making recommendations based on guest preferences. English is widely spoken, making the experience accessible to international visitors. Service can slow during peak hours when the venue reaches capacity.

Overall: ★★★★½

Jing-A Brewing Co. represents the best of Beijing's contemporary food and beverage scene—internationally informed but locally rooted, innovative while respecting tradition. For visitors seeking to experience the city beyond its historic sites, Jing-A offers a window into the creative energy of modern Beijing.
