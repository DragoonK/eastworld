About Mizuno

Mizuno (also known as "Micchan" to locals) is one of Osaka's oldest and most respected okonomiyaki establishments, serving authentic Osaka-style savory pancakes since 1945. This family-run institution has been listed in the Michelin Guide's Bib Gourmand category, recognizing its exceptional food at moderate prices. Located in the heart of Dotonbori, Mizuno is renowned for its premium ingredients and time-honored techniques.

An Okonomiyaki Legacy Since 1945

Mizuno stands as a living testament to Osaka's okonomiyaki tradition, continuing to serve exceptional versions of this iconic dish after nearly eight decades in business. Founded in 1945 in the aftermath of World War II, the restaurant has survived through Japan's most transformative periods while maintaining its commitment to quality and tradition.

Still operated by the Mizuno family, now in its third generation, this modest establishment has earned international acclaim while remaining true to its roots. The restaurant's enduring popularity speaks to both its historical significance and the consistently exceptional quality of its food. Despite its Michelin recognition and international fame, Mizuno maintains the welcoming, unpretentious atmosphere that characterizes Osaka's food culture.

The Art of Osaka-Style Okonomiyaki

Mizuno specializes in Osaka-style okonomiyaki (sometimes called Kansai-style), which differs from Hiroshima-style by mixing all ingredients into the batter before cooking. What distinguishes Mizuno from countless other okonomiyaki establishments is their meticulous attention to ingredients and technique.

Their signature dish, the Yamaimo-yaki, incorporates grated mountain yam into the batter, creating an exceptionally light and fluffy texture that contrasts beautifully with the crisp exterior. Every okonomiyaki is prepared with precision by skilled chefs right before your eyes on the teppan (iron griddle), allowing you to witness the artistry that goes into each dish.

Premium ingredients feature prominently in all their offerings – from the high-grade Kagoshima pork belly and fresh seafood to the specially selected cabbage and dashi. The restaurant makes their own tare (okonomiyaki sauce) according to a secret family recipe, creating a distinctive flavor that can't be found elsewhere.

"Okonomiyaki is Osaka's soul food, and at Mizuno, we honor this tradition by focusing on quality above all else. Our recipe has been refined over three generations, but the essence remains the same – creating okonomiyaki that brings joy to those who eat it. That's the Osaka way." - Mizuno Family

Menu Highlights

Mizuno's menu focuses on perfecting a select range of okonomiyaki variations:

  • Yamaimo-yaki (¥1,550) - Their signature dish featuring grated mountain yam in the batter for exceptional fluffiness, with pork belly
  • Yamaimo-yaki Special (¥1,980) - The signature with an added premium selection of seafood (shrimp, squid, and scallops)
  • Mizuno Special (¥1,730) - The house specialty combining pork, shrimp, squid, and kimchi for a slightly spicy flavor profile
  • Negi-yaki (¥1,500) - A thinner variation with abundant green onions and tender pork
  • Modanyaki (¥1,580) - "Modern-yaki" style with yakisoba noodles incorporated into the batter
  • Dotonbori-yaki (¥1,880) - A generous seafood-focused variation with a variety of fresh catches

Additional items and accompaniments:

  • Yakisoba (¥1,100) - Stir-fried noodles prepared on the same teppan
  • Chilled Beer (¥650) - Perfect pairing with the rich flavors of okonomiyaki
  • Sake Selection (from ¥750) - Curated to complement their dishes

Eastworld's Review

Food Quality: ★★★★★

Mizuno sets the gold standard for okonomiyaki in Osaka. Their Yamaimo-yaki's texture is nothing short of sublime – light and fluffy inside with the perfect crisp exterior. Every ingredient shows thoughtful selection, from the cabbage's sweetness to the premium quality meats and seafood. Their house-made sauce and mayo provide distinctive flavor that elevates the entire experience.

Atmosphere: ★★★★

The restaurant maintains an authentic, unpretentious atmosphere despite its fame. Seating at the teppan counter offers an engaging dining experience as you watch skilled chefs craft each dish with remarkable precision. The space is modest and can feel crowded, but this adds to its authentic charm rather than detracting from it.

Service: ★★★★

Service is efficient and friendly, with staff managing the constant flow of diners with practiced ease. While language barriers can present minor challenges for international visitors, the staff makes a genuine effort to accommodate everyone. The chefs' skill and performance at the teppan add an additional dimension to the service experience.

Value: ★★★★

While prices are higher than at typical okonomiyaki shops, the quality fully justifies the cost. The portions are generous, and the premium ingredients and masterful preparation make this a worthwhile culinary investment. The Bib Gourmand recognition from Michelin correctly identifies Mizuno as offering exceptional quality at reasonable prices.

Location: ★★★★★

Situated in the heart of Dotonbori, Osaka's famous food district, Mizuno is perfectly positioned for visitors exploring the area. Its central location makes it easily accessible from major transit hubs and tourist attractions, though this convenience also contributes to the frequent lines.

Overall: ★★★★★

Mizuno represents okonomiyaki at its finest – a perfect demonstration of why this dish is considered Osaka's soul food. For visitors seeking an authentic and exceptional okonomiyaki experience, Mizuno delivers a meal that will become a highlight of any Osaka trip. Its combination of historical significance, culinary excellence, and authentic atmosphere make it essential for food enthusiasts.

Tips for Visitors

  • Arrive 15-30 minutes before opening time to minimize waiting, especially on weekends
  • Late afternoon (3-5 PM) typically sees shorter lines than peak lunch and dinner hours
  • Counter seating offers the best view of the chefs' artistry at the teppan
  • The Yamaimo-yaki is their signature dish for good reason - don't miss it
  • Portions are generous; consider sharing if you want to try multiple varieties
  • Ask the chefs questions if you're curious about the preparation - they appreciate genuine interest
  • Taking photos is permitted, but be respectful of other diners and the chefs' work
  • English menus are available, making ordering accessible for international visitors
