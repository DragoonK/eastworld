TOKYO

Discover Japan's dynamic capital, where ancient traditions meet futuristic innovation in a city that never sleeps.

Tokyo, Japan's bustling capital, is a mesmerizing blend of the ultramodern and the traditional, from neon-lit skyscrapers to historic temples. The city is a global powerhouse for technology, fashion, food, and culture, offering an unparalleled urban experience that stimulates all the senses.

A sprawling metropolis of 37 million people, Tokyo is organized into distinct neighborhoods, each with its own character: Shinjuku's towering skyscrapers and bustling nightlife, Shibuya's youthful energy and famous pedestrian crossing, Asakusa's ancient temples and traditional crafts, and Ginza's luxury shopping and fine dining. Despite its vastness, Tokyo maintains a sense of order, cleanliness, and efficiency that makes navigating this incredible city a surprisingly pleasant experience.

Senso-ji Temple

Asakusa 24 hours (Main hall: 6:00-17:00)

Tokyo's oldest temple, founded in 628 AD, featuring the iconic Kaminarimon Gate and a long shopping street (Nakamise-dori) leading to the main temple complex.

Tokyo Skytree

Sumida 10:00-21:00

The tallest structure in Japan (634m) offering breathtaking panoramic views of Tokyo from its two observation decks, along with shops and restaurants.

Shinjuku Gyoen

Shinjuku 9:00-16:30 (Closed Mondays)

One of Tokyo's largest and most beautiful parks featuring three distinct garden styles: Japanese traditional, formal French, and English landscape, with seasonal highlights including cherry blossoms.

Meiji Shrine

Shibuya Sunrise to sunset

A serene Shinto shrine dedicated to Emperor Meiji and Empress Shoken, set within a 170-acre evergreen forest with 120,000 trees representing species from all over Japan.

Akihabara Electric Town

Akihabara Varies by store

The world-famous electronics district and center of anime and manga culture, featuring countless shops selling electronics, games, anime merchandise, and maid cafes.

GETTING AROUND

Local Transportation Tips

The Tokyo Metro and JR lines form an extensive network covering the entire city. Get a Suica or PASMO IC card for seamless travel across all systems.

The JR Yamanote Line loops around central Tokyo, connecting most major districts including Shinjuku, Shibuya, Tokyo Station, and Ueno.

Buses are less convenient for tourists due to complex routes, but useful in areas not covered by trains. Many tourist areas have hop-on-hop-off sightseeing buses.

Taxis are clean, safe, and plentiful but expensive. Most drivers don't speak English, so have your destination written in Japanese or show them on a map.

Many neighborhoods like Shibuya, Shinjuku, and Asakusa are best explored on foot, with pedestrian-friendly streets and numerous hidden gems in side alleys.

The metro system typically runs from around 5:00 AM to midnight. Plan accordingly for late nights, or be prepared to take a taxi if staying out after the last train.
