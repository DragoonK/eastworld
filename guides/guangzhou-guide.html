GUANGZHOU

Where ancient heritage meets stunning modernity in China's vibrant southern metropolis

Guangzhou, historically known in the West as Canton, is the capital and largest city of Guangdong Province in southern China. As one of China's three largest cities and a major transportation hub, Guangzhou blends its 2,200 years of history with rapid modern development.

Once the starting point of the ancient Maritime Silk Road, today's Guangzhou is a global commercial center renowned for its twice-yearly Canton Fair, cutting-edge architecture, and Cantonese cuisine—considered by many to be China's finest. The city's unique location near Hong Kong and Macau makes it an essential destination for understanding China's economic development and southern culture.

TOP ATTRACTIONS

Canton Tower

Haizhu District 9:30-22:30

The 600-meter tall Canton Tower dominates Guangzhou's skyline, offering breathtaking views, a glass observation deck, and the world's highest horizontal Ferris wheel.

Guangzhou Opera House

Tianhe District 10:00-16:30

Designed by architect Zaha Hadid, this stunning example of modern architecture resembles two pebbles on the Pearl River and hosts world-class performances.

Shamian Island

Liwan District Always accessible

A historic sandbank island with tree-lined avenues and colonial European architecture that once housed foreign trading companies. Now a peaceful retreat from the bustling city.

Chen Clan Ancestral Hall

Yuexiu District 9:00-17:00

Built in 1894, this magnificent complex showcases traditional Chinese architecture and houses the Guangdong Folk Art Museum with impressive wood carvings, pottery, and embroidery.

Qingping Market

Liwan District 8:00-18:00

China's largest traditional medicine market and a vibrant trading hub where you can find everything from herbs and spices to dried seafood and exotic teas.

Temple of the Six Banyan Trees

Yuexiu District 8:30-17:00

A 1,500-year-old Buddhist temple featuring the iconic Flower Pagoda, ancient sculptures, and a serene atmosphere in the heart of the bustling city.

WHERE TO EAT

Panxi Restaurant

Liwan District $$$

A historic restaurant established in 1947 set in a beautiful garden with pavilions and ponds, serving authentic Cantonese dim sum and classic dishes.

Taotao Ju

Liwan District $$

A century-old dim sum institution where trolleys laden with bamboo steamers circle the dining room offering a vast selection of traditional dim sum items.

Huangsha Seafood Market

Liwan District $$

Select fresh seafood from the market and have it cooked to your specifications at the surrounding restaurants for the ultimate fresh dining experience.

Liuhua Restaurant

Yuexiu District $$$

Famous for its Cantonese hot pot, this upscale restaurant serves premium ingredients in a refined setting, featuring the region's distinctive broth and accompaniments.

Modern and stylish hotel with colorful design, convenient access to shopping and transportation, plus an energetic social scene with live music.

GETTING AROUND

Local Transportation Tips

Guangzhou's extensive metro system has 13 lines covering most major attractions. It's clean, efficient, and signs are in both Chinese and English.

The city has a comprehensive bus network, though route information may be limited in English. The Yang Cheng Tong card works for both metro and buses.

Taxis are plentiful and relatively inexpensive. Have your destination written in Chinese characters to show the driver, as many don't speak English.

Pearl River cruises offer a scenic way to view the city's impressive skyline, particularly beautiful when illuminated at night.
