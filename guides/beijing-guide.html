BEIJING

Explore China's magnificent capital, where ancient imperial grandeur meets modern innovation in a city of extraordinary contrasts.

Beijing, the sprawling capital of China, stands as both a monument to Chinese civilization's ancient glory and a showcase of its rapid modernization. With a history spanning three millennia, the city has served as the seat of power for various dynasties and governments, accumulating layer upon layer of cultural heritage that makes it one of the world's most historically significant cities.

Home to seven UNESCO World Heritage Sites including the Forbidden City and Great Wall, Beijing offers visitors an unparalleled journey through Chinese history while simultaneously presenting a vibrant contemporary metropolis of 21 million people. Massive ring roads, gleaming skyscrapers, and Olympic venues stand alongside centuries-old hutong alleyways, imperial palaces, and tranquil temples, creating a fascinating urban tapestry where tradition and innovation coexist in dynamic equilibrium.

TOP ATTRACTIONS

Forbidden City (Palace Museum)

Central Beijing 8:30-17:00 (Closed Mondays)

The world's largest palace complex and China's most significant building, this Ming Dynasty imperial palace served as the home of 24 emperors. Its 980 buildings and 8,700 rooms showcase unparalleled Chinese architectural design and imperial collections.

Great Wall of China (Mutianyu Section)

70km Northeast of Beijing 7:30-17:30

This fully-restored section of the Great Wall offers spectacular views with fewer crowds than Badaling. Dating back to the Northern Qi dynasty, the Mutianyu section features densely packed watchtowers and is accessible via cable car or a challenging hike.

Temple of Heaven

Dongcheng District 8:00-17:30

A masterpiece of Ming dynasty architecture where emperors performed rituals to ensure good harvests. The complex features the striking Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests, Imperial Vault of Heaven, and Circular Mound Altar, set in a beautiful 267-hectare park.

Summer Palace

Haidian District 6:30-18:00

A vast ensemble of lakes, gardens, and palaces that served as the imperial summer retreat. Dominated by Longevity Hill and Kunming Lake, this UNESCO site showcases Chinese landscape garden design with temples, pavilions, bridges, and the famous marble boat.

Nanluoguxiang Hutongs

Dongcheng District Open 24 hours

Explore Beijing's traditional residential lanes dating back to the Yuan Dynasty. This well-preserved hutong area offers a glimpse into old Beijing life with its courtyard homes (siheyuan), now interspersed with boutique shops, cafes, and small museums.

798 Art District

Chaoyang District 10:00-19:00

A thriving arts quarter housed in a complex of decommissioned military factory buildings. This creative hub features contemporary art galleries, studios, design firms, restaurants, and cafes in spaces that retain their Bauhaus-inspired industrial architecture.

WHERE TO EAT

Quanjude Roast Duck

Multiple locations ¥¥¥

Founded in 1864, this iconic restaurant is synonymous with Beijing's most famous dish: Peking duck. The ducks are roasted in traditional brick ovens over fruitwood to achieve the signature crispy skin and tender meat, served with thin pancakes and condiments.

Wangfujing Snack Street

Dongcheng District ¥

A bustling food street offering a wide array of traditional Chinese snacks and street food, from tanghulu (candied fruit skewers) and jianbing (savory crepes) to more adventurous options like scorpion skewers and starfish on sticks.

Siji Minfu

Dongcheng District ¥¥

A popular local chain famous for its Peking duck at reasonable prices, along with Beijing specialties like zhajiang noodles (noodles with bean sauce) and shredded pork with sweet bean sauce. The restaurant near the Forbidden City's east gate offers views of the palace.

TRB Hutong

Dongcheng District ¥¥¥¥

Set in a beautifully restored 600-year-old temple complex, this fine dining establishment offers contemporary European cuisine with impeccable service. The restaurant features seasonal tasting menus that showcase premium ingredients with artistic presentation.

WHERE TO STAY

Aman Summer Palace

Haidian District 5-star luxury

A peaceful retreat housed in historic buildings that once accommodated guests of the Empress Dowager Cixi. The resort features private access to the Summer Palace through a secret door, traditional courtyard architecture, and exquisite Chinese decor.

The Peninsula Beijing

Wangfujing 5-star luxury

Recently renovated luxury hotel combining contemporary style with Chinese artistry, located near major shopping areas and the Forbidden City. All-suite accommodations feature cutting-edge technology, while the hotel's art gallery showcases works by Chinese artists.

The Orchid Hotel

Gulou Hutong Boutique hotel

A charming boutique hotel set in a traditional hutong neighborhood, offering a blend of Chinese architecture and contemporary design. The property features courtyard gardens, rooftop terraces with city views, and personalized service for an authentic Beijing experience.

Rosewood Beijing

Chaoyang District 5-star luxury

Contemporary luxury hotel in the Central Business District featuring spacious rooms with floor-to-ceiling windows, six restaurants and lounges, an indoor pool, and one of the city's largest spas. Art displays throughout showcase works by Chinese artists.

GETTING AROUND

Local Transportation Tips

Beijing's extensive subway system is the world's busiest and most efficient way to navigate the city, with 24 lines covering most attractions. Trains run from 5:00 AM to 11:00 PM, with fares starting at ¥3.

The city has over 1,000 bus routes, though navigation can be challenging for non-Chinese speakers. Major routes like tour bus line 1 (¥10) stop at popular attractions including the Summer Palace and Temple of Heaven.

Taxis are affordable and plentiful, with a starting fare of ¥13 for the first 3km. Always use official taxis (colored yellow or green with a light on top) and have your destination written in Chinese to show the driver.

Ride-hailing apps like Didi Chuxing (China's Uber) offer an easy alternative to street taxis, with an English interface available. Payment requires a Chinese payment method or cash.

Bike-sharing services like Mobike and Ofo provide a fun way to explore flat Beijing. Download the app, scan the QR code on the bike, and pay a small fee (typically ¥1-2 per 30 minutes).

Beijing's size makes walking impractical for covering large distances, but areas like the hutongs, Forbidden City, and Nanluoguxiang are best explored on foot for an authentic experience.
