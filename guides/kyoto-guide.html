KYOTO

Experience the heart of traditional Japan in the ancient capital, where temples, shrines, and gardens create a living museum of Japanese culture.

Kyoto, Japan's ancient imperial capital for over 1,000 years, remains the country's spiritual and cultural heart. With its 1,600 Buddhist temples, 400 Shinto shrines, magnificent palaces, and traditional wooden townhouses (machiya), Kyoto offers visitors an immersive journey into traditional Japanese culture and aesthetics.

Despite being a modern city of 1.5 million people, Kyoto has preserved its historical districts and traditional ways of life. The city is surrounded by mountains on three sides, creating a natural setting that changes dramatically with the seasons — from spring's cherry blossoms to autumn's fiery maple leaves. This combination of cultural depth, natural beauty, and living traditions makes Kyoto an essential destination for understanding the soul of Japan.

TOP ATTRACTIONS

Fushimi Inari Taisha

Southern Kyoto 24 hours, free admission

Famous for its thousands of vermilion torii gates forming a network of trails leading into the wooded forest of Mount Inari, dedicated to the Shinto god of rice and business.

Kinkaku-ji (Golden Pavilion)

Northern Kyoto 9:00-17:00

A stunning Zen Buddhist temple covered in gold leaf, set beside a reflective pond in a classical Japanese garden, originally built in 1397 as a retirement villa for shogun Ashikaga Yoshimitsu.

Arashiyama Bamboo Grove

Western Kyoto 24 hours, free admission

A magical path through towering bamboo stalks that create an otherworldly atmosphere, particularly beautiful when sunlight filters through the dense bamboo. Located near Tenryu-ji Temple and the scenic Arashiyama district.

Kiyomizu-dera

Eastern Kyoto 6:00-18:00

Founded in 778, this UNESCO World Heritage site features a wooden stage extending from the main hall, offering spectacular views of the city. The temple is named after the Otowa Waterfall's pure waters that flow within the complex.

Gion District

Central Kyoto Best in early evening

Kyoto's famous geisha district with preserved machiya townhouses, traditional tea houses, and exclusive ryotei restaurants. Visitors might glimpse geiko (Kyoto's geisha) and maiko (apprentice geisha) on their way to evening appointments.

Philosopher's Path

Eastern Kyoto 24 hours, free admission

A scenic stone path following a canal lined with cherry trees, named after philosopher Nishida Kitaro who used this route for daily meditation. Especially beautiful during cherry blossom season, connecting Ginkaku-ji and Nanzen-ji temples.

WHERE TO EAT

Nishiki Market

Central Kyoto ¥-¥¥

Known as "Kyoto's Kitchen," this 400-year-old covered shopping street features over 100 stalls selling local specialties, fresh seafood, pickles, dried goods, and Japanese sweets, with plenty of options for sampling as you explore.

Nakamura

Central Kyoto ¥¥¥¥

Three Michelin-starred restaurant serving traditional Kyoto cuisine (kaiseki) in an elegant setting, led by chef Motokazu Nakamura, the 6th generation of his family to run this 200-year-old establishment.

Tempura Endo

Gion ¥¥¥

Experience the art of tempura at this refined restaurant where seasonal ingredients are lightly battered and fried to perfection before your eyes, served as part of a multi-course meal alongside elegant side dishes.

Ippudo Ramen Kyoto

Downtown Kyoto ¥¥

Branch of the famous ramen chain serving delicious Hakata-style tonkotsu ramen with rich pork broth and thin noodles, located in a beautiful machiya townhouse with both counter and table seating.

WHERE TO STAY

Tawaraya Ryokan

Central Kyoto Traditional luxury ryokan

One of Japan's oldest and most exclusive ryokans (traditional inns), operating for over 300 years and hosting emperors, celebrities, and diplomats. Features impeccable service, tatami rooms, private gardens, and exquisite kaiseki meals.

The Ritz-Carlton Kyoto

Kamogawa Riverfront 5-star luxury

Stunning luxury hotel blending contemporary design with traditional Japanese aesthetics, situated along the Kamogawa River with views of the Higashiyama mountains. Features spacious rooms, Japanese gardens, spa, and excellent dining options.

Shunkoin Temple Stay

Myoshinji Temple Complex Temple lodging

Experience authentic Zen Buddhist temple lodging with meditation sessions led by the English-speaking vice abbot. Simple but comfortable accommodations in a 400-year-old temple with beautiful gardens and cultural activities.

Kyoto Granbell Hotel

Gion District 4-star boutique hotel

Stylish boutique hotel in the heart of the Gion district with Japanese-inspired modern design, rooftop terrace, public bath, and convenient access to Kyoto's main attractions and traditional entertainment areas.

GETTING AROUND

Local Transportation Tips

Kyoto has two subway lines (Karasuma and Tozai) that intersect at Karasuma Oike Station. The Karasuma Line runs north-south along Karasuma Street, while the Tozai Line runs east-west.

City buses are the most comprehensive way to reach major sights. Consider a one-day bus pass (¥700) for unlimited travel. Routes 100 and 101 cover most tourist destinations.

JR lines and private railways connect different parts of Kyoto and neighboring cities. The JR Sagano Line is useful for reaching Arashiyama, while the Keihan Line serves eastern Kyoto.

Kyoto's flat terrain makes cycling an excellent option. Many hotels and rental shops offer bicycles for around ¥1,000 per day, with dedicated cycling paths along the Kamogawa River.

Many of Kyoto's districts like Higashiyama, Gion, and Arashiyama are best explored on foot. Walking allows you to discover hidden temples, gardens, and traditional shops not accessible by public transport.

Taxis are plentiful and convenient for reaching destinations not well-served by public transport, especially in the evening. Drivers are reliable and usually understand major tourist destinations by name.
