SIEM REAP

Discover the gateway to Cambodia's ancient wonders, where timeless temples and vibrant culture create an unforgettable journey into the heart of the Khmer Empire.

Siem Reap, once a sleepy Cambodian village, has transformed into a vibrant tourist hub that serves as the gateway to the magnificent temples of Angkor, one of the most important archaeological sites in Southeast Asia. The city beautifully balances its role as custodian of Cambodia's ancient Khmer heritage with a thriving contemporary scene of boutique hotels, world-class dining, and creative arts.

Beyond the breathtaking temple complexes that draw millions of visitors annually, Siem Reap offers travelers an authentic glimpse into Cambodian life. From the bustling markets and floating villages to the traditional dance performances and evolving food scene, the city provides a perfect blend of cultural immersion and modern comfort. With its friendly locals, laid-back atmosphere, and rich historical significance, Siem Reap has rightfully earned its place as one of Southeast Asia's most captivating destinations.

TOP ATTRACTIONS

Angkor Wat

Angkor Archaeological Park 5:00-17:30

The crown jewel of Khmer architecture, this 12th-century temple complex is the largest religious monument in the world. Originally built as a Hindu temple dedicated to Vishnu, it gradually transformed into a Buddhist temple. Don't miss the spectacular sunrise over its iconic silhouette.

Bayon Temple

Angkor Thom 7:30-17:30

Known for its 216 enigmatic stone faces of Avalokiteshvara, this late 12th-century temple represents the creative genius of King Jayavarman VII. The temple's three-tiered structure is adorned with extensive bas-reliefs depicting historical events and everyday life during the Khmer Empire.

Ta Prohm

Angkor Archaeological Park 7:30-17:30

Made famous by the film "Tomb Raider," this temple has been left largely as it was found, with massive tree roots growing over and through the ancient stone structures. The atmospheric combination of jungle and ruins creates a truly magical experience unlike any other temple in Angkor.

Pub Street & Old Market Area

Central Siem Reap Open 24 hours

The lively heart of Siem Reap comes alive at night with bustling bars, restaurants, and nightclubs. The adjacent alleyways and Old Market (Phsar Chas) offer excellent shopping opportunities for souvenirs, textiles, art, and street food, creating a vibrant cultural hub.

Kampong Phluk Floating Village

Tonle Sap Lake 7:00-18:00

Located on the edge of Tonle Sap Lake, this fascinating village consists of houses built on 6-7 meter high stilts that protect against seasonal flooding. Boat tours through the village and flooded forest provide insight into a unique way of life that has adapted to the lake's extreme water level changes.

Cambodia Landmine Museum

25km from Siem Reap 7:30-17:30

Founded by ex-child soldier Aki Ra, this museum tells the sobering story of Cambodia's landmine problem through exhibits of deactivated mines and educational displays. Entry fees support relief programs for landmine victims and the museum's rehabilitation center.

WHERE TO EAT

Malis Restaurant

Pokambor Avenue $$$

Founded by Master Chef Luu Meng, Malis serves sophisticated Cambodian cuisine with a focus on reviving traditional recipes that were nearly lost during the Khmer Rouge era. The elegant riverside setting and beautifully presented dishes make this a premier dining destination in Siem Reap.

Cuisine Wat Damnak

Wat Damnak Village $$$

Helmed by French chef Joannès Rivière, this acclaimed restaurant offers innovative tasting menus that showcase Cambodian ingredients and techniques with French influence. The constantly changing menu reflects seasonal availability from local markets, farms, and foraged ingredients.

Chanrey Tree

Pokambor Avenue $$

Set in a traditional wooden house with a beautiful garden, Chanrey Tree offers authentic Khmer cuisine focusing on family recipes and fresh, local ingredients. The menu includes both familiar dishes and lesser-known regional specialties, served in a refined yet relaxed atmosphere.

Genevieve's Restaurant

Near Old Market $$

This socially responsible restaurant donates a significant portion of profits to local community projects. The menu offers a mix of Cambodian and international dishes prepared with high-quality ingredients. Known for exceptional service, it's consistently top-rated on travel sites.

WHERE TO STAY

Raffles Grand Hotel d'Angkor

Royal Garden 5-star luxury

A landmark since 1932, this historic luxury hotel blends colonial charm with modern amenities. Set amid lush tropical gardens with Cambodia's largest swimming pool, the property features antique-filled rooms, the famous Elephant Bar, and impeccable service that has hosted royalty and celebrities.

Shinta Mani Angkor

French Quarter 5-star boutique

Designed by acclaimed architect Bill Bensley, this stylish boutique hotel combines contemporary design with Khmer influences. The property features a refreshing pool, spa, and excellent dining options. A portion of profits supports the Shinta Mani Foundation's community development projects.

Jaya House River Park

Riverside 5-star boutique

This eco-conscious boutique hotel offers just 36 rooms, two swimming pools, and exceptional personalized service. The property is renowned for its plastic-free initiative and community involvement. Each room features Khmer-inspired art and design elements with modern luxury amenities.

Viroth's Hotel

Wat Bo Area 5-star boutique

A sleek 1950s-inspired design hotel with 35 rooms centered around a stunning courtyard pool. The hotel perfectly balances retro-chic aesthetics with modern luxury and exemplary service. Features include a spa, restaurant, and rooftop terrace with city views.

GETTING AROUND

Local Transportation Tips

Tuk-tuks are the most popular and convenient way to travel around Siem Reap and to nearby temples. Expect to pay $5-8 for transportation within town, $15-20 for a day at the main Angkor temples, and $25-30 for further sites. Always agree on a price before departing.

Bicycles are an excellent option for exploring the town and even the closer temples (the main Angkor complex is within cycling distance). Many hotels offer free or low-cost bicycle rentals, or you can rent from shops in town for $2-5 per day.

Air-conditioned taxis are available for longer journeys or day trips to distant temples. A full day of temple exploration costs around $35-45. Apps like PassApp and Grab are available in Siem Reap for convenient booking.

Private drivers with cars or vans can be arranged through your hotel for maximum comfort and flexibility, particularly useful for full-day temple tours. Expect to pay $45-80 per day depending on distance and vehicle type.

Central Siem Reap is quite walkable, with most restaurants, markets, and shops clustered around the Old Market area and riverfront. Walking is ideal in the cooler early mornings and evenings, though midday heat can be intense.

For visiting floating villages on Tonle Sap Lake, boat tours are the only option. These can be arranged through your hotel, tour agencies in town, or at the boat docks. Prices vary by village and tour length ($20-35 per person).
