SHANGHAI

Discover China's vibrant global metropolis, where futuristic skylines meet colonial heritage in a thrilling symphony of East and West.

Shanghai, China's largest city and global financial hub, stands as a testament to the nation's rapid rise and cosmopolitan ambitions. Situated on the Yangtze River Delta where it meets the East China Sea, this city of over 26 million people represents China's window to the world and its vision for the future.

With its iconic skyline dominated by the Oriental Pearl Tower and Shanghai Tower, the city seamlessly blends ultra-modern architecture with its historical European concessions and traditional Chinese elements. From the colonial-era buildings of the Bund to the ancient gardens of Yuyuan, and from the vibrant street life of Tianzifang to the luxury shopping of Nanjing Road, Shanghai offers visitors an intoxicating blend of experiences that captures both China's rich heritage and its ambitious future.

TOP ATTRACTIONS

The Bund (Waitan)

Huangpu District Open 24 hours

This iconic waterfront promenade stretches along the Huangpu River, offering spectacular views of the Pudong skyline. The west side features 52 beautifully preserved buildings in various architectural styles including Gothic, Romanesque, and Art Deco, representing Shanghai's colonial past.

Yu Garden (Yuyuan)

Huangpu District 8:30-17:00

This exquisite Ming dynasty garden built in 1559 showcases classical Chinese garden design with rockeries, pavilions, ponds, and halls. Surrounding the garden is the bustling Yuyuan Bazaar, perfect for sampling local snacks and shopping for souvenirs.

Shanghai Tower

Pudong New Area 8:30-21:30

China's tallest building and the world's second-tallest skyscraper features the world's highest observation deck at 546 meters. Its unique twisted design reduces wind loads and symbolizes China's emergence as a global power, offering breathtaking 360° views of the city.

Tianzifang

Huangpu District 10:00-22:00

A labyrinth of narrow alleyways formed by traditional shikumen architecture now housing hip boutiques, art studios, craft shops, and trendy cafes. This arts and crafts enclave preserves the residential atmosphere of the old Shanghai longtang neighborhoods.

Nanjing Road

Huangpu District 10:00-22:00 (varies by store)

One of the world's busiest shopping streets stretching 5.5km from the Bund to Jing'an Temple. The pedestrian section offers everything from traditional Chinese brands to international luxury labels, department stores, restaurants, and street performers.

Former French Concession

Xuhui District Open 24 hours

A charming district characterized by plane tree-lined avenues, European architecture, and village-like atmosphere. Explore colonial mansions, boutique shops, stylish restaurants, hidden cafes, and art galleries while soaking in Shanghai's most beautiful neighborhood.

WHERE TO EAT

Din Tai Fung

Multiple locations ¥¥

World-famous Taiwanese chain renowned for its perfect xiaolongbao (soup dumplings). Watch skilled chefs craft each dumpling through glass-walled kitchens, ensuring each perfect pocket contains exactly 18 pleats and the perfect amount of soup and filling.

Jia Jia Tang Bao

Huangpu District ¥

A no-frills local institution for xiaolongbao with perpetual lines out the door. This humble eatery serves what many consider the city's best soup dumplings with pork, crab-pork, and other fillings at incredibly reasonable prices.

Wei Xiang Zhai

Huangpu District ¥

A Shanghai institution famous for its cong you ban mian (scallion oil noodles), the quintessential Shanghainese comfort food. This simple dish of chewy noodles coated in scallion-infused oil and soy sauce is elevated to perfection at this tiny, perpetually crowded eatery.

Ultraviolet by Paul Pairet

Secret location ¥¥¥¥¥

The city's most avant-garde dining experience: a 20-course sensory feast where just 10 diners are transported to a secret location. Each course is paired with lights, sounds, music, and scents in a fully immersive experience that has earned three Michelin stars.

WHERE TO STAY

The Peninsula Shanghai

The Bund 5-star luxury

Elegant Art Deco-inspired hotel with a prime location on the historic Bund, offering spectacular views of the Pudong skyline. Features spacious rooms with cutting-edge technology, exquisite dining options, a luxurious spa, and indoor pool.

The Waterhouse at South Bund

Huangpu District Boutique hotel

Award-winning design hotel housed in a converted 1930s warehouse. This architectural gem juxtaposes the building's industrial past with minimalist modern design. Features 19 unique rooms, a popular rooftop bar with stunning Pudong views, and the acclaimed Table No. 1 restaurant.

Capella Shanghai, Jian Ye Li

Former French Concession 5-star luxury

Luxurious all-villa urban resort set within a restored 1930s shikumen (stone gate) estate in the heart of the former French Concession. Each villa combines Ming dynasty-inspired decor with contemporary comforts and private courtyards or terraces.

W Shanghai - The Bund

North Bund 5-star luxury

Vibrant, design-forward hotel offering playful interpretations of Shanghai's past and future. Features dramatic views of the Huangpu River and Pudong skyline, innovative dining concepts, a luxurious AWAY spa, and the brand's signature Whatever/Whenever service philosophy.

GETTING AROUND

Local Transportation Tips

Shanghai's extensive metro system is one of the world's largest with 16 lines covering most tourist attractions. Clean, efficient, and affordable (¥3-10 per trip), with all signs and announcements in both Chinese and English.

The Maglev train connects Pudong International Airport to Longyang Road Station in just 8 minutes at speeds up to 430 km/h. From there, transfer to the metro to reach downtown. One-way fare: ¥50 (standard) or ¥40 (with same-day flight ticket).

Taxis are plentiful and relatively affordable, with starting fares of ¥14 for the first 3km. Look for official taxis (typically turquoise or red) with a light on top, and always ask for the meter to be turned on. Having your destination written in Chinese is helpful.

DiDi (China's equivalent of Uber) offers convenient ride-hailing through its app, which has an English interface. Payment requires a Chinese payment method or cash. The app shows estimated fare before booking.

Bike-sharing apps like Meituan (yellow bikes) and Hello (blue bikes) offer an easy way to explore the city. Download the app, scan the QR code on the bike, and pay a small fee (typically ¥1.5-2 per 30 minutes).

Many of Shanghai's most interesting areas are best explored on foot, particularly the Former French Concession, The Bund, and the pedestrianized sections of Nanjing Road. Walking allows you to discover hidden gems and local life beyond the tourist spots.
